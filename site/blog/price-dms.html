Instagram & DMs

"How much for lips?" How to reply and actually get the booking

Reply fast, give a "from" price and always end with a next step. Plus the wording to avoid in the UK.

Samuel Ayo, Founder of AYSTO · 12 October 2026 · 4 min read

It's the most common DM an aesthetics clinic gets, and the easiest one to lose.

Someone sees your results on Instagram, sends "how much for lips?" at 11pm, and waits. If the answer comes the next afternoon, they've often already booked somewhere that replied first.

Reply fast, even if it's short

Speed matters more than a perfect answer. A quick reply with a starting price and a next step beats a long, careful one tomorrow.

Give a "from" price

People asking for prices are usually comparing. "It depends, come in for a consultation" can feel like you're hiding something, and they'll go to the clinic that gave them a number.

A "from" price with a reason it varies is honest and keeps the conversation going:

Lip filler starts from £X. Your practitioner will confirm what's right for you at a free consultation, so there's no pressure to decide now.

Always end with a next step

Don't end on the price. End with a question that moves them forward, and make it easy to say yes:

I've got Thursday at 6:30 or Saturday at 11 for a consultation. Would either of those work?

Two specific times work better than "when suits you?", because the person only has to pick one.

Watch the wording

In the UK, prescription-only medicines can't be advertised to the public. That's why most clinics say "anti-wrinkle treatment" rather than naming the product, in DMs as well as ads.

Leave clinical questions for the consultation. If someone mentions pregnancy, breastfeeding, medication or a medical condition, thank them, note it, and pass it to your practitioner. Don't answer it in the DM.

Make it happen at 11pm

Most clinics can't have someone watching Instagram every evening. That's where an AI assistant helps: it replies in your tone, gives the prices you've approved, offers real slots from your diary and hands anything clinical to your team.

The rules above still apply. You decide what it says, and it follows them every time, even at 11pm on a Sunday.
